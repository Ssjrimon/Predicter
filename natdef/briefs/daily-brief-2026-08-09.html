The bottom line

The Gulf's security architecture changed on Friday without the United States in it. Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement — an armed attack on one treated as an attack on all — extending a mutual-defence commitment from the only nuclear-armed Muslim state to the Gulf's largest oil exporter. It was signed in Islam's holiest city after roughly a year of negotiation, and after eighteen months in which Gulf states absorbed repeated Iranian missile and drone attacks.

Against that, the Hormuz breakthrough has not arrived. President Trump said on Tuesday night that we would know within 48 hours; four days on there is no joint statement, and Tehran has restated that agreeing a route is not the same as reopening the strait. Meanwhile the Houthis widened their front against Saudi Arabia on Friday. Iran's thread moves from escalating to volatile — genuine movement in both directions at once.

01
Iran / Hormuz

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan sign a collective-defence pact in Mecca

The three states signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement on 7 August, stipulating that an armed attack against any one shall be regarded as an attack against them all. It was signed by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, President Erdoğan and Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who was accompanied by army chief Asim Munir. The text extends to Turkey a commitment Saudi Arabia and Pakistan established bilaterally in the Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement of 17 September 2025. Riyadh's deputy minister for public diplomacy said the pact threatens no country in the region.

The combination is unusual: NATO's second-largest military, the world's largest oil exporter, and the only Muslim-majority nuclear weapons state. Coverage has widely called it NATO-style, and the collective-defence clause supports the comparison — but Atlantic Council analysts argue it is not a NATO-like alliance and does not constitute a new regional bloc, noting there is no integrated command, no treaty organisation and no standing force behind it. Both readings are on the table; the text itself is one clause, not a structure.

So what The 2026 National Defense Strategy's third line of effort asks partners to carry more of their own defence. This is that — but arranged among themselves rather than under American auspices, and reportedly driven by Gulf doubts about the United States as security guarantor after eighteen months of absorbing Iranian strikes. Burden-shifting and burden-sharing look identical on a spending chart and are not the same thing.

Joint statement published by the Saudi Press Agency and Pakistan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Name appears as both "Joint Defence" and "Joint Deterrence" across outlets; the ministries use Defence.

02
Iran / Hormuz

The 48-hour window closed without a joint statement

President Trump told reporters in Los Angeles on Tuesday night that talks with Iran were moving along very nicely and that we would know within 48 hours. That window expired Thursday. As of this morning there is no Iran–Oman joint statement. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei has said the text is in final review and drafting provided certain third parties do not obstruct the process — a formulation he applied to the United States. Foreign Minister Araghchi said Saturday that Washington continues to violate the June memorandum. Tehran and Washington are passing messages through intermediaries rather than negotiating directly.

The mechanism itself came into clearer focus. Iran's semi-official Fars agency reported Friday that Iranian and Omani routes would remain in temporary use before traffic is redirected to a central corridor, with Iranian officials overseeing inbound traffic and Omani officials outbound. That reconciles what looked like a contradiction — Baghaei said on 2 August the route would be neither the northern nor the southern one, while terms reported on 5 August described northern and southern lanes. Those refer to different phases. It remains unclear whether Oman or Washington has accepted the arrangement Fars describes, and Iran maintains it negotiates only with Muscat.

So what Yesterday's brief carried the joint statement as a horizon item due within days, on a 50-50 estimate. It did not land. The forecast is logged as a miss. The structural obstacle has not moved since February: Iran wants transit fees and sovereign control of the waterway; the United States and the Gulf states insist on free navigation under the Law of the Sea. A route can be agreed without touching that.
03
Terrorism

Houthi front against Saudi Arabia widens

Houthi forces struck a Saudi base inside Yemen and targets in Najran, a Saudi region on the border, on Friday — the same day the Mecca pact was signed. This follows the maritime blockade of Saudi Arabia the group declared on 22 July and repeated missile attacks on Saudi tankers through early August. Iranian-backed proxies are now engaging Saudi Arabia directly on land and at sea.

The 2026 Annual Threat Assessment, written in March, described Iran's proxy network as severely degraded following the 2024–25 campaigns against Hizballah and the Huthis. The degradation is real and the group is still opening a second front against the region's largest military spender five months later.

So what Degraded is not defeated, and the two are being conflated. The Houthi capability that survived Operation ROUGH RIDER is sufficient to blockade a G20 economy. That is the practical test of the burden-sharing premise, and the Mecca pact is one answer to it.
04
Homeland / Hemisphere

Haiti's election date is on the calendar and disputed by its own prime minister

Yesterday's horizon carried Haiti's elections as scheduled for this month. Checking it produced a more contested picture. The Provisional Electoral Council's published calendar sets the first round for 30 August, with materials distribution running 12–28 August and a second round on 6 December. Around 300 political parties or groups have registered. But Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé said in May that security conditions are clearly not met for an August vote, and reference sources describe the timetable as already delayed by him over gang violence and funding. The Transitional Presidential Council's mandate expired on 7 February with no succession plan in place.

The backdrop is severe: more than 8,000 killed in the past year's violence, over 1.4 million internally displaced, and Haitian police responsible for more than 3,200 deaths, at least 18% of them people not affiliated with armed groups, per UN figures cited by Freedom House. The 2026 ATA separately reports gangs firing on the US Embassy compound in Port-au-Prince since September to disrupt the vote.

So what Whether this election happens on 30 August is genuinely unresolved, and yesterday's brief presented it as settled. Corrected here. It is the one dated horizon item on the board where the responsible official and the official calendar disagree in public.
