Iran / HormuzLate file — 9 Aug

Iran names IRGC veteran Mohsen Rezaei as new SNSC secretary

Iran's Supreme National Security Council named Mohsen Rezaei, 71, as its new secretary, replacing Mohammad Baqer Zolqadr. Rezaei commanded the IRGC from 1981 to 1997, spent more than two decades as secretary of the Expediency Discernment Council, and is the subject of an Interpol red notice tied to the 1994 AMIA bombing in Buenos Aires. He now also serves as Supreme Leader Khamenei's representative to the body that coordinates Iran's security and foreign policy under President Pezeshkian.

This was announced 9 August and prominently reported 10 August — before Brief 003's own cutoff — and was not caught in that sweep. Corroborated across US News, Al-Monitor, Al Jazeera and IranWire; the underlying appointment is an Iranian state announcement, run as attributed-claim rather than verified-primary.

So what A harder-line, more publicly outspoken figure now sits atop the body coordinating Iran's security and foreign policy during the Hormuz negotiation — a personnel change inside the same institution setting the preconditions this brief has tracked since Brief 002.

US News, Al-Monitor, Al Jazeera, IranWire — 9–10 August 2026.

Terrorism

Suspected Houthi strike kills three crew on a Bab el-Mandeb cargo ship

UKMTO confirmed a strike Tuesday on a small cargo vessel transiting the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, saying crewmembers were injured, "some fatally," and that the incident remains under investigation. Multiple outlets — Washington Times, Insurance Journal (citing Lloyd's List Intelligence and LSEG) and CNN's live coverage — reported three dead: two Pakistani nationals and one Indonesian. The vessel is reported, with a "if confirmed" hedge in the sourcing closest to the incident, as the Egyptian-owned cargo ship Tihamah, which had departed Yemeni government-held al-Mokha on 8 August.

Houthi responsibility is suspected, not claimed or independently adjudicated at the time of this brief. If the toll and attribution hold, these are the first shipping deaths since the Iran war began 28 February — a threshold the Houthi campaign, including its 20 July blockade declaration against Saudi Arabia, had not crossed until now.

So what Both hedges — Houthi attribution and the vessel's identity — are carried here rather than resolved. Even so, a first fatal hit on commercial shipping changes the risk calculus for any operator still transiting the strait. TR moves to 7, still escalating.

UKMTO statement, via Washington Times and Insurance Journal, 11 August 2026; CNN live coverage, 11 August 2026.

02
Energy / Markets

Hormuz shipping traffic falls to a five-week low

Kpler data, cited consistently by The Hill, The National and Times of Israel (Reuters), show Strait of Hormuz transits falling from a roughly 10-day average of 11–12 vessels a day to six on Sunday and seven on Monday — a five-week low — as hopes of a near-term US-Iran reopening deal continue to fade. The pre-war baseline was 130–140 ships a day before Iran closed the strait when the war began 28 February.

This is a volume-side data point alongside the price-side move (Brent's fourth straight gaining session) logged in Brief 003; both are reported by the same underlying dynamic — a stalled reopening — rather than treated as separate developments.

So what Traffic, not just price, now confirms the market read: this is being priced and routed around as a prolonged closure, not a lapse before an imminent deal.

The Hill, The National, Times of Israel (Reuters) — all citing Kpler, 11 August 2026.
