LF1TRLATE FILE · 24 Aug

LATE FILE -- Two ships hit on 24 Aug at opposite ends of the shipping war: a Houthi missile on a Saudi tanker off Yanbu, an unclaimed projectile off Oman

Yemen's Houthis said they struck the Saudi-flagged tanker Amzan, operated by the national shipping company Bahri, with a ballistic missile in the Red Sea off Yanbu on 24 August, claiming an accurate hit that started a fire. Bahri confirmed a maritime incident and said all crew were safe with no injuries; UKMTO independently reported a tanker struck by an unknown projectile 63 nautical miles west of Yanbu. Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry did not respond to Al Jazeera's request for comment. The fire itself rests on the Houthi account alone.

The same day, at roughly 00:25 local, UKMTO reported an unidentified projectile striking the engine room of a vessel about 9 nautical miles northeast of Ash Shishan, Oman, near the Strait of Hormuz, disabling it. Crew were reported safe. No party claimed responsibility and UKMTO assigned none. Both incidents fell inside Brief 011's window; neither ran in it.

So what. Both fell inside Brief 011's window and neither ran in it. Together they show the maritime campaign is being prosecuted at both chokepoints at once, and that attribution is now the exception -- only one of the two has a claimant.

01RU25–27 Aug

CIA Director Ratcliffe went to Moscow on 25 Aug; the Kremlin denied it, then confirmed it and said Putin was briefed

CBS News first reported that CIA Director John Ratcliffe was in Moscow on Tuesday 25 August. Axios confirmed it citing two US officials and called it the highest-level Trump administration visit to Russia since January; flight-tracking showed a US military C-17 leaving Riga at 05:50 GMT and landing in Moscow around 07:04 GMT. Asked that same day about US-envoy talks and about a US military aircraft in Moscow, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said: “No, there are no such plans,” and that he had no such information. The Kremlin subsequently confirmed the visit and said Putin had been briefed on the outcome. The CIA declined to comment throughout.

On what was said, the record is thinner and further from origin. The New York Times reported on 27 August, sourced to current and former US officials, that Ratcliffe told FSB director Aleksandr Bortnikov that Moscow's military and economic position would deteriorate further without serious peace negotiations — arguing that the life expectancy of a Russian soldier at the front was “less than 35 minutes” and that Russia had taken about 1% of Ukraine's territory in 18 months. CNN reported the same day that he also warned Russia against attacking NATO and pressed Moscow to cut Iran off. Those figures are one party's argument to a counterpart, not measured statistics, and this brief reaches them two hops from origin.

So what. The highest-level US visit to Russia since January, run through an intelligence channel rather than a diplomatic one, and initially denied by the Kremlin's own spokesman. An intelligence back-channel that both capitals now acknowledge is a different instrument from a negotiating round, and it is the first of either found in this ledger's coverage.

02IR27 Aug

Qatar's PM ran the first structured Hormuz mediation since the Islamabad memorandum lapsed -- and Iran's price is unchanged

Qatar's Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani met Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, President Masoud Pezeshkian, Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf and security chief Mohsen Rezai in Tehran on 27 August. The parties reviewed a Hormuz framework with real mechanics in it: a temporary shipping corridor, possible joint mine-clearing, and terms for resuming US–Iran dialogue. Reuters-sourced coverage reported Tehran agreed to draw up a list of conditions after the Qatari emissary pressed Iran to respect freedom of navigation.

Iran's price did not move. Rezai: “the US must first take practical steps to fulfil Iran's conditions and then Iran will proceed to open the Strait of Hormuz” — blockade lifted, assets unfrozen, oil sanctions removed, strait afterward. That is the same sequencing Iran set out on 10 Aug when it added sanctions relief and reparations to the agreed route (Brief 003). Trump, for his part, said he was “not in a hurry” to resume negotiations. No signed text was found this sweep.

So what. The framework is concrete (temporary corridor, joint mine-clearing) but Rezai's sequencing demand is the same maximalist position Iran held on 10 Aug: blockade lifted, assets unfrozen, sanctions removed, and only then the strait. A live channel with an unmoved price is progress in form, not in substance.

03EN25–28 Aug

The largest sanctions package of the war landed on 24 Aug and Brent fell more than 5% that week

Brent settled at $89.31 on Friday 28 August, down 0.43% on the day and more than 5% on the week; WTI settled at $83.40, down more than 4% on the week. Trading Economics separately quoted Brent at $88.29 the same day. The two levels differ by about a dollar and are both given here rather than averaged; the direction and size of the weekly move agree. CNBC reported on 25 August that oil hit a one-week low as investors shrugged off the new sanctions — that piece is cited by headline only, its full text having returned a 403.

The week that produced this decline is the week “Operation Economic Outcast” took effect — 50-plus entities, an explicit warning to China, the largest package of the war. Phil Flynn of Price Futures Group pointed instead at “rumors we might see a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend.” Janiv Shah of Rystad Energy and John Evans of PVM Oil Futures were quoted in the same coverage. Brief 011 could not confirm a Brent figure at all; this closes that gap and inverts the direction that brief expected.

So what. The market is pricing the mediation rumour, not the sanctions -- which closes the price gap Brief 011 could not confirm and inverts the expected direction. It also means a failed mediation is now a price risk in a way it was not a week ago.

04RU28 Aug

A second irreplaceable Tu-95MS destroyed at Engels-2 in six weeks, then a Russian ICBM test three days after Ratcliffe left

Ukraine's Security Service Special Operations Centre “A” struck Engels-2 strategic bomber base in Saratov Oblast overnight into 28 August. Satellite imagery released by the AviVector analyst group and reported 29 August shows a Tu-95MS with part of its right wing broken off, assessed as likely beyond repair. The same unit destroyed another Tu-95MS at Engels on 16 July. Russia has not confirmed either loss; the damage assessment is third-party imagery analysis, not an official statement from either side. ISW's 28 August assessment logs the strike alongside continued Ukrainian refinery strikes and 164 Russian drones launched overnight.

Three days after Ratcliffe left Moscow, Russia's Defence Ministry announced a mobile ICBM launch from Plesetsk to the Kura range on Kamchatka, over 6,700km: “All assigned tasks were completed in full,” on a “flawless trajectory.” The ministry did not name the missile; reporting identifies it as likely the RS-24 Yars. Separately, Zelenskyy used his 28 August evening address to set a goal of 1,000 drones a day against Russia, roughly triple the current rate — “There needs to be more. There is a concrete goal, and we will fulfil this task” — while thanking Washington for briefing Kyiv on the Moscow meetings. Kyiv Post reports Washington asked Ukraine to stop striking Moscow and St Petersburg; Engels and Yaroslavl are neither, so the public record does not establish that request was breached.

So what. Both sides escalated in the strategic register during the same week they opened a channel. Ukraine is attriting a bomber fleet Russia cannot replace; Russia answered with the one signal that costs it nothing and is unmistakable.

05HLCORRECTION · 23 Aug

CORRECTION -- the Haiti attack Brief 011 logged as ~30 dead on 24 Aug killed 47 on 23 Aug at Kenscoff, with 52 taken hostage

Brief 011 logged a gang coalition killing at least 30 people in a Haitian farming community on 24 August. Three independent fetches this sweep — Al Jazeera 27 August, UPI 26 August, UN News 28 August, all tracing to the UN Integrated Office in Haiti — establish instead that the attack came on the night of 23 August at Kenscoff, a hillside community outside Port-au-Prince, killing at least 47 including five children, injuring 22, and abducting more than 52.

UPI names Izo 2, alias Didi, as leading the group in Kenscoff under Johnson André's Viv Ansanm coalition. He posted video of nearly 20 captives and threatened to kill them if security operations continued: “If one of my men or a soldier dies, these people will be killed.” Police chief Vladimir Paraison said an investigation is underway into whether the attack was enabled by complicity or negligence. The Security Council called an emergency session for 28 August. The UN's quarterly figures record 1,408 killed, 656 injured and 326 subjected to sexual violence in gang violence between April and June.

So what. The error came from exactly where Brief 011 said the risk was: wire convergence read without a full fetch, under a disclosed rate limit. The hostages, and an emergency Security Council session on 28 Aug, are the live part.
