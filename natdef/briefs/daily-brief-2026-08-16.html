So what Brief 006's sovereignty framing survives, but it now rests on Hegseth and Bessent rather than on the President's remark, which the White House itself declines to stand behind. The correction runs here rather than into Brief 006, which is not edited.

01
Iran / Hormuz

The Islamabad memorandum's 60-day window expires with no extension and no negotiation

The 14-point memorandum of understanding negotiated in Islamabad and signed on 17 June — the interim framework meant to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, extendable by mutual consent — reached the end of its 60-day window with neither side giving ground and neither willing to extend it. A senior Iranian source told Reuters that Tehran has not discussed an extension with Washington at all, because it does not consider the 60-day agreement to have begun. Trump, speaking in New York on Friday, said "There's nobody to negotiate with," urged Americans to accept paying "a little bit more" for fuel, and again described the blockade as a "wall of steel." Iran's deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi replied that the waterway "was Iran's, is Iran's, and will remain Iran's." Political economist Nader Itayim wrote that "the MOU died weeks ago."

One account cuts against this, and it is not harmonised away. On 12 August, Anadolu — carried by Middle East Monitor — reported Pakistani government sources close to the mediation saying both sides had "conveyed their consent to the mediators to extend the deadline" and were exchanging messages on the length. Four days later The National, citing an Iranian official through Reuters, reports that Iran never entered such talks. Both are anonymous and they cannot both be true; the later account is more specific and is consistent with the principal's own on-the-record words. Separately, Treasury Secretary Bessent's promised measures — "economic isolation the world has never seen" — remain announced but unpublished for a second week, and Iran had pressed for Lebanon, where 11 people were killed by Israeli strikes on 15 August, to be treated as integral to the agreement.

So what Every reopening scenario this ledger has carried since June ran through this framework, and nothing has been announced to replace it — not Pakistan, which brokered both the April ceasefire and the June memorandum, not Oman, which holds the separate route talks, and not Qatar, which had urged Gulf coordination on implementation. With no live channel the thread stops oscillating and starts pointing one way: IR moves from volatile to escalating at unchanged intensity 9. Intensity is held because no new kinetic threshold was crossed today; only the diplomatic one.

The National, 16 August 2026, 17:51 UTC (Jihan Abdalla), citing a senior Iranian source to Reuters; contradicted by Anadolu via Middle East Monitor, 12 August 2026, 11:55 UTC. Bloomberg's same-day framing corroborates the expiry reading. contested.

02
Russia / Ukraine

Records in both directions: ~600 drones toward Moscow, Kyiv's book market burns, a Flamingo strike hits Samara's satellite-rocket plant

Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said some 600 drones were detected heading toward the Russian capital overnight and that 201 were destroyed over the Moscow region — reported as Ukraine's largest wave against Moscow of the war. Russia's Ministry of Defence said it destroyed 822 Ukrainian drones nationwide. Moscow region governor Andrey Vorobyov said an 83-year-old man was killed when a drone struck a private home; a Wildberries warehouse burned. Russia's answer used 106 drones and several missile types against Kyiv, Poltava and Dnipropetrovsk, setting fire to the Pochaina book market in Kyiv's Obolonskyi district, the city's largest. Casualty totals do not reconcile across outlets and are not combined here: AP and NPR carry "at least six killed in Russia," AFP and France 24 carry "16 killed in Russia and Ukraine," and the Guardian's briefing carries "two killed and 16 injured" in Ukraine. They count different geographies over different windows.

The more consequential item is the target set. President Zelenskyy said Ukrainian Flamingo cruise missiles struck a rocket plant at Samara, roughly 900 km from the border, which produces launchers for Rassvet — the broadband satellite constellation Russia is building as an alternative to Starlink. Ukraine has spent 2026 striking refineries; this reaches a different class of target, the space-launch industrial base. The strike is Zelenskyy's account of his own operation and has no Russian confirmation in this sweep. AP's framing that reciprocal long-range attacks have pushed civilian deaths to their highest level since the war's first months in 2022 is wire analysis rather than a counted figure, and is carried as such.

So what With the front near standstill and talks frozen, the war has migrated to deep strike, and both sides are now setting their own records at it inside a single night. RU moves 7 → 8, still volatile — the divergence this ledger identified on 15 August has not resolved, it has widened, and a Ukrainian reach into Russia's satellite-launch industry is a rung above the refinery campaign that has defined its long-range effort so far.

Sobyanin, the Russian MoD, Gov. Vorobyov, Ukraine's Air Force and President Zelenskyy — every figure is a belligerent's account of its own air defence or its own strike, none independently verified. Via AP/NPR, AFP/France 24, the Irish Times/Guardian briefing, CNN, Kyiv Independent and Euromaidan Press, 16 August 2026. contested.

03
Energy / Markets

Kharg Island's western terminal restarts after 25 days as Iran reroutes around the blockade

Maritime intelligence firm Windward said Kharg Island's western oil terminal was reactivated on 12 August, with satellite imagery showing a 333-metre dark VLCC berthed and loading for the first time since 18 July, having waited offshore since 11 July. The eastern terminal and the LPG facility stayed empty and 16 to 17 tankers remained in the offshore waiting area. TankerTrackers.com separately reported a National Iranian Tanker Company VLCC loading about two million barrels at the Azarpad jetty. Windward recorded the North Larak anchorage cluster rising to 35 hulls on 12 August from 28 two days earlier, roughly 74 percent of them dark and ten of them sanctioned, and said Russia-to-Iran wet-cargo movements across the Caspian rose 2.9 times — to 23 shipments from eight — in the 164 days after 28 February, with volumes going from 174,100 to about 437,000 barrels. "Iran isn't waiting for the blockade to lift. It's rerouting around it," Windward said.

Two cautions travel with it. Reuters notes that a single VLCC loading at a terminal handling about 90 percent of Iran's oil exports is a signal, not proof that normal exports have resumed. And TankerTrackers offers a competing reading of the same data: Iranian production appears roughly in line with domestic refinery runs, which would mean Iran may simply not need to export as much — reduced demand for export capacity rather than restored capacity. Both readings fit; neither is resolved here. Oil markets were closed for the weekend, with Brent last settled at $88.52 on Friday, up 5.9 percent on the week and carrying what Goldman Sachs' spot fair value implies is roughly a 10.7 percent risk premium.

So what This cuts against the story the price has been trading on. The blockade has severely disrupted Iran's main export artery without closing it, and the adaptation — dark tonnage at North Larak, Caspian inflows from Russia, a partial Kharg restart — is structural rather than opportunistic. EN is held at 8, volatile, precisely because the competing reading fits the same evidence; a thread should not move on data that supports two opposite conclusions.

Windward and TankerTrackers.com satellite/AIS analysis, read at one hop via The National, 16 August 2026, 11:49 UTC; Kharg background from Reuters and a JP Morgan note citing Kpler. Commercial firms, not a government finding. attributed-claim.

Europe / NATO

A NATO fighter downs a drone in Romanian airspace — the fourth Romania has shot down this year

Romania's Ministry of National Defence said surveillance systems detected an aerial vehicle entering Romanian airspace from Moldova at about 04:44 local time, roughly 24 km north of Galați. A Spanish Air Force F-18 flying NATO air-policing duties established radar contact, received permission to engage, and shot the drone down at 05:01. Debris fell in an unpopulated area near the Ukrainian border; no injuries or property damage were reported.

The Russian attribution is cut. Outlets characterise it as a "suspected Russian drone," but the ministry statement as reached does not name Russia, the drone entered from Moldova rather than from Ukraine, and Romanian authorities have previously attributed some incursions to electronic interference rather than deliberate targeting. What is on the record is the fourth such shoot-down by Romania this year, on the same morning that Russia and Ukraine exchanged their largest long-range salvos of the war.

So what A NATO aircraft opening fire inside allied airspace is now a recurring event rather than an incident, and that normalisation is the finding. Europe/NATO is deliberately not moved, holding at 5, escalating: a fourth occurrence of an established pattern is tempo, not a new threshold, and the thread was already raised on 15 August for the Warsaw plot and the Baltic warnings.

Romanian Ministry of National Defence, read at one hop via Ukrainska Pravda, UPI, CBS News and Euronews, 16 August 2026. attributed-claim; the Russian-origin attribution does not clear the gate and is cut.
