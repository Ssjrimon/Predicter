Items

01IR · Iran / Hormuz

The war restarted on 1 September, and the mediation channel that defined Brief 012's window is dead

US forces struck Iranian targets on 1 September, the first direct clash in about a month. Trump said the strikes answered a failed Iranian attempt to mine the Strait of Hormuz and to target a US base in Jordan. CENTCOM described the target set as air defence sites, radar systems, maritime assets and facilities, mine-laying capabilities and communications sites.

Iran alleges one strike hit a house during a wedding celebration in Sirik, in the south. Al Jazeera reported five killed; NBC News reported at least four, including two children; a local hospital said 70 people were admitted, 11 still under treatment and five in intensive care. Some accounts place the strike at Kuhestak rather than Sirik. Vice President Vance said he was 'extremely skeptical' it hit a wedding party and said he would not call the conflict a war; CENTCOM spokesman Tim Hawkins said US forces never target civilians, unlike the IRGC. Iran's foreign ministry called it a war crime. None of this is established here: the claim, the rebuttal and the discrepancy between figures all run, named.

So what — Brief 012 led on the first structured Hormuz mediation since the Islamabad memorandum lapsed. That channel is now gone and the exchange is back. The mediation was the thing holding the intensity judgment at 'volatile'; without it the direction is one-way.

02IR · Iran / Hormuz

Iran's retaliation reached five countries hosting US facilities: Kuwait, Bahrain, the UAE, Jordan and Iraq

Iran's military said it attacked US bases in Kuwait and the UAE on the morning of 3 September, naming satellite communications equipment, warehouses and fighter hangars at Ahmad al-Jaber Air Base in Kuwait, and troop positions and radar at al-Minhad in the UAE. Gulf News additionally names Ali Al Salem in Kuwait and Sheikh Isa in Bahrain among the targets, and reports Iranian strikes on Jordan and Iraq in the same sequence.

Kuwait's army said its air defences were engaging hostile missiles and drones and told residents that explosions they heard were interceptions. Critical Threats assesses the Artesh conducted the attack as part of an effort to raise the cost of the US campaign. Under this ledger's own filing rule these are events in a theatre and file to IR, not to AL: no change in who is obligated to defend whom has been found, and AL is held.

So what — Hosting a US installation is now what draws the strike. That is the concrete form of the question this ledger has tracked on AL since Brief 012 — Gulf states asking what the American guarantee is worth — and it arrived as incoming fire rather than as a communiqué.

03EN · Energy / Markets

The headline Hormuz throughput number is now itself contested between the US government and commercial ship trackers

Treasury Secretary Bessent said at least 10 million barrels a day were getting through, and 15 to 17 million on Monday. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC more than 17 million transited on Monday. Trump said the Navy was helping about 30 ships through each night. The US military reported escorting 40 vessels carrying 18 million barrels on Tuesday — a wartime high, on a day it also struck 60 targets.

Against that, Lloyd's List Intelligence counted about 14 non-Iranian-linked ships a day over 17-23 August, and the Joint Maritime Information Center's advisory of 1 September described commercial traffic as far below baseline despite a modest uptick from recent lows. EIA data put second-quarter flows at 4.9 million barrels a day against 21.6 million in the fourth quarter of 2025. Al Jazeera's analysis notes the gap cannot be closed without knowing how the US tallies its own figure, and that an operational count plausibly includes vessels that moved near the naval convoy corridor without completing a transit — which would not be dishonest, but would not be the same measure Lloyd's is reporting either.

So what — This is the number every reader uses to judge whether the strait is working, and it can no longer be quoted as a fact without saying who is counting. A brief that picks a side here is guessing; the honest product is the gap itself.

04RU · Russia / Ukraine

The first reciprocal restraint of the war, and a three-hour Kremlin meeting that produced no announced framework

Putin ordered no strikes on Kyiv for three days from midnight on 5 September, tied to the visit of Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. Zelenskyy said Ukraine would refrain from strikes on Moscow with immediate effect through the end of 7 September. Neither side's compliance can be verified from open sources at this cutoff.

The envoys met Putin at the Kremlin for more than three hours on 5 September and flew on to meet Zelenskyy in Kyiv on 6 September. Peskov said beforehand there was 'no talk of new ideas', and afterwards called the Kyiv talks useful with no sign of a breakthrough. Zelenskyy said he expects the war to continue. Putin had said on 3 September that there was a chance of an agreement while adding that Ukrainian threats against Russian aviation made one harder, and that there would be 'no negotiations with terrorists'. The thread is held at 8/volatile: movement in both directions inside one window is exactly what that value is reserved for.

So what — A mutual pause on striking capitals is a real, checkable commitment rather than a statement of intent, and it is the first of its kind found in this ledger. It also expires on 7 September, which makes the next 24 hours the test of whether anything was actually agreed.

05EU · Europe / NATO

Germany formally blamed Russia for the Leipzig airport drone attack and moved to close two Russian missions

An explosive-laden drone fell near a Ukrainian cargo aircraft at Leipzig/Halle airport in early August and failed to detonate; a police bomb squad defused it, and two further drones were found at or near the airport over the following days and weeks. After a nearly month-long investigation, Berlin publicly blamed Russia on 1 September, citing what it described as the signatures of a Russian hybrid operation.

Germany will close Russia's consulate general in Bonn by 18 September, terminate the leases on the Russian House for Science and Culture in Berlin, and tighten immigration scrutiny of Russian citizens. Russia called the suggestion absurd, and Putin accused Germany of planting the evidence. Both run: the attribution is Germany's and the denial is Russia's, and neither is adopted here.

So what — A NATO member has publicly attributed an attempted explosive attack on its own soil, against a supply line into Ukraine, and acted on the attribution diplomatically. That is a different category from the sabotage incidents this thread has been logging, and it moves EU to 6.

06HL · Homeland / Hemisphere

Haiti's Gang Suppression Force mandate expires on 30 September and the government has asked for renewal

The Haitian government publicly called on 4 September for renewal of the international anti-gang task force. The Gang Suppression Force was authorised for an initial twelve months on 30 September 2025; its mandate expires on 30 September 2026, and the US and Panama, the Security Council's penholders on Haiti, are expected to seek re-authorisation during September.

The request follows the Kenscoff attack of 23 August, which this ledger corrected in Brief 012 to at least 47 killed and more than 50 abducted. First-round presidential and legislative elections remain scheduled for 13 December 2026, with a possible runoff on 21 February 2027.

So what — A hard, dated deadline inside the next month, on the only international security instrument operating in Haiti, ten weeks before a first-round election that gangs have said publicly they intend to prevent.
